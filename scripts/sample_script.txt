THE LAST LIGHTHOUSE

INT. LIGHTHOUSE LANTERN ROOM - DUSK

The lamp has been dark for three nights. MARA, the keeper, climbs the spiral stair with a toolbox that is far too heavy for her.

MARA: Three nights. Three nights and not one ship has called.

She kneels at the burner and pries the housing open. Inside, the wick is fused into a black knot.

MARA: You stubborn old thing.

A knock echoes up the tower. MARA freezes.

EXT. LIGHTHOUSE DOOR - DUSK

TOBIAS, a fisherman with salt in his beard, stands in the doorway holding a lantern of his own.

TOBIAS: The harbour sent me. They say you have given up.

MARA: The harbour can row out and fix it themselves.

TOBIAS: I brought oil. And I brought Pip.

PIP, a small girl in an oversized coat, steps out from behind him and holds up a spool of fresh wick.

PIP: I made it myself. Out of my winter socks.

INT. LIGHTHOUSE LANTERN ROOM - NIGHT

The three of them work together. TOBIAS pours the oil. PIP threads the wick. MARA strikes the match.

MARA: Stand back.

The flame catches and the great lens throws a blade of gold across the water.

EXT. THE SEA - NIGHT

Far out on the black water, a fishing boat turns its bow toward the light and answers with a horn.

PIP: They heard us.

MARA: They always do. Eventually.

The camera pulls back from the tower until the light is a single bright star on the coast.
